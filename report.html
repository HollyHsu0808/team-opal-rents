What will this flat rent for? — Team-Opal

Microburbs hackathon, 7 November 2026 · data profile: real · every number below is computed on real adverts dated on or before the stated cut-off; nothing dated after 31 July 2026 is used anywhere.

1. What we found

  • Held-out result (window B, 2,898 adverts, Jun–Jul 2026, trained on adverts ≤ 31 May): median absolute error $45.0, 77% within $100, R² 0.88 — against $56.1 / 69% for the simple floor estimate.
  • A flat's own earlier advert is the strongest evidence: $40.0 median error on 2,151 adverts, against $60.0 where only the building or the suburb is known.
  • Old evidence is worse than no evidence. On window A, using a flat's last advert when it is more than 36 months old gave $53.1 overall; replacing those stale prices with the building's current comparables gave $46.5. The same-building rule was selected on A and frozen before B.
  • Changing the bedroom count between two adverts of the same address (whole-building ids, split lettings) multiplied the error by about 2.4 in our window-A check; own history is therefore only used when the bedrooms match.
  • Asking rents sit on a price grid (in the past-leases file 84% end in $0, 52% are multiples of $50, 26% of $100): snapping predictions to the nearest $5 improved window-A error from $46.5 to $45.0.
  • LiDAR (NSW Spatial Services, 2013 and 2020) gives every building's height (coverage 100%, 79 buildings built between the two surveys) and a floor-by-floor view test: water is visible from 15% of floors overall, 6% of floors 1–2 and 60% of floors above 20.
  • Same building, same bedrooms, different floor: within building × bedroom groups (22,593 flats), a flat whose floor sees water in every direction asks 16% more than one that sees none (park view +6%, clear of nearby roofs +5%), measured with LiDAR line-of-sight per floor. Building location, age and quality cancel out in this comparison. Pricing that floor-view difference against the comparable flats (P1) kept window-A error at $45 and lifted the share within $100 from 79.1% to 79.2%; on window B it was neutral ($45, 77.1%, R² 0.877 → 0.880). P1 was chosen on A after the base rule was frozen and B was evaluated for both; both B results are shown.
  • Public-data honesty line: the cross-sectional LiDAR / flight-path / age adjustment was rejected on A: not applied: building-level layers double-count what same-building comparables already contain (on A, water view +25%, age −6% per decade against suburb medians, yet applying them raised error). Our layers describe physical exposure; they are not causal estimates.
  • Flats within 1 km of a Sydney Airport 16/34 approach path rent -1.6% against the model, others -1.0% (window-B residuals; a geometric proxy, not a noise measurement).
  • Residual by LiDAR water view (window B): none -1.0% (n=1,924), some (<=25%) -1.6% (n=344), wide (>25%) -0.6% (n=33).

2. Date split (no random splits)

Window A (train ≤ 30 Apr 2026, test May) is used once to choose between the candidate rules and to freeze the settings. Window B (train ≤ 31 May, test Jun–Jul) is run exactly once with the frozen rule and is the number we report. The final model is refit on everything ≤ 31 Jul 2026 and predicts the 65,777 flats for Aug–Sep. For every window, the rent index, the suburb medians, the parking premium and the building comparables are rebuilt from that window's training adverts only; the code asserts that no training row is dated after the cut-off.

4. Method

  1. Market index: a repeat-rent index from pairs of adverts of the same flat (location and quality cancel out), solved monthly with smoothing, plus a shrunk suburb deviation. The level is held at the cut-off month; no uplift is extrapolated into Aug–Sep (Sydney unit asking rents were flat to slightly down after mid-2026).
  2. Own history: the flat's latest advert with the same bedroom count, moved to the cut-off month with the index, with the parking premium added or removed for the other output column.
  3. No usable own advert: suburb × bedrooms × parking medians of the last 12 months (≥10 adverts), falling back to suburb × bedrooms, then bedrooms.
  4. Building comparables: adverts of other flats in the same building with the same bedrooms (last 36 months), index- and parking-adjusted, the target flat excluded; one-flat-one-vote collapses each neighbour's adverts to one signal and shrinks toward the ladder by the number of distinct flats.
  5. Stale own history: when the own advert is older than the frozen threshold, the building comparables / ladder estimate replaces it.
  6. Snapping: predictions rounded to the asking-rent grid when window A showed it helps.
  7. Public layers (LiDAR floor views, roofline, height, flight path, new build, strata age): fitted on A training residuals and applied only if A improves; otherwise reported as tested and not used.

5. Data

Training adverts: 153,040 valid of 235,718 raw; adverts dated after the cut-off dropped: 0. Fit for the reported window: train end 2026-05-31, 149,523 training adverts, 57,914 repeat pairs for the index, 49,209 flats with own history, citywide parking premium 0.323 (log).

Public layers were pre-computed from open data (LiDAR 1 m height grids, 4,399 OSM residential building footprints, per-floor line-of-sight to water and parks, distance to runway centrelines, strata-plan age) and joined to flats by building address and coordinates. All public layers predate every cut-off.
